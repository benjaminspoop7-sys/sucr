Squiggle progress — Material 3 Expressive, on Sucr's surfaces

M3 Expressive's wavy indicator is a stroked path, not a filled bar: amplitude is exposed as a fraction of height, the wave is parameterised by wavelength, and it travels one wavelength per second by default. Two behaviours matter more than the shape: the amplitude collapses to zero at ≤ 10 % and ≥ 95 % (Compose source), and the track is drawn with the same wave as the fill. Adopted with Sucr's numbers; refused wherever a wave would claim data it does not have.

1 — The rule one element may move on its own; it is the only one that encodes nothing but time

The wave says alive. It is therefore only allowed on aggregates — the things you watch — and never on the map, whose cells encode which block has which network's bytes. Length still encodes exactly the percentage, so the wave argues with nothing. The amplitude flattening at the ends is not a decoration: a download at 8 % and one at 97 % are both about to change state, and the indicator settles before it does. Our defaults: 3 px amplitude at 18 px height, 20 px wavelength, 4 px stroke, one wavelength per second. At 240 px wide the bar holds twelve periods.

A 72 px ring (r 29) has a 182 px circumference; at M3's ~20 dp wavelength that is nine periods, which reads as noise at this diameter. Six periods (30 px of arc) keep the ripple legible and keep the arc's endpoints where the percentage says they are. The ring travels at the same one wavelength per second; it is shown still here because this page is a specimen sheet, not the app.

7 — Not taken three places the squiggle would lie

Wave on the map cells A cell means "this block, these networks, this share". At 8 px a 3 px amplitude eats most of the edge, and the hedge notch — the one shape that separates two racing bands at 1.10 — would disappear into the ripple. The map stays rigid.
Wave on the track If the empty part moves too, the ruler moves with the thing being measured. The track is a fixed reference; only the filled length travels.
Wave plus shimmer plus gradient One moving element per surface. The wave already says "alive"; a shimmer on top of it says it twice, and the motion budget in §6.3 has no room for two cues competing over the same pixels.
